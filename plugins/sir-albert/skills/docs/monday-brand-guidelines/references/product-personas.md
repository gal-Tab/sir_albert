# Monday.com Product Personas

Deep-dive on each product's brand personality, target audience, competitive positioning, and voice character with extensive examples.

## Overview

Each Monday.com product targets different audiences and has a distinct brand personality while maintaining the core Monday.com DNA (confident, fluff-free, playful, passionate).

## Work Management Persona

### Target Audience

**Primary**:
- Operations Directors
- Project Managers (PMO)
- Team Leads managing multiple projects
- C-level executives (VP Operations, COO)

**Characteristics**:
- Manage teams of 10-500+
- Responsible for multiple workflows/departments
- Value scalability and customization
- Make strategic platform decisions
- Budget authority or influence

**Pain Points**:
- Teams using scattered tools
- Lack of visibility across projects
- Manual status reporting
- Can't scale current processes
- Inflexible software

### Competitive Positioning

**Monday.com Work Management is**:
- The **leader**, not a challenger
- **Premium** but accessible (not enterprise-only)
- **Trustworthy** for critical operations
- **Flexible** enough for any workflow

**We compete with**: Asana, ClickUp, Smartsheet, Airtable, Wrike

**Positioning statements**:
- "The work platform trusted by 150,000+ teams"
- "Built for scale, designed for ease"
- "One platform for every team and workflow"

### Voice Character

**Visionary**: Forward-thinking, strategic perspective
- Speak to where teams are going, not just where they are
- Reference trends, growth, scale
- Paint picture of efficient operations

**Knowledgeable**: Expert guidance without condescension
- We've seen it all, we know what works
- Share best practices naturally
- Confident in our solutions

**Premium**: High quality, polished, professional
- Avoid casual slang
- Use proper grammar consistently
- Speak to decision-makers, not just users

**Trustworthy**: Reliable, stable, dependable
- No overpromising
- Specific results and metrics
- Real customer stories

## CRM Persona

### Target Audience

**Primary**:
- Sales Development Reps (SDRs)
- Account Executives
- Sales Managers
- Revenue Operations

**Characteristics**:
- Hit quotas under pressure
- Tired of clunky CRMs (Salesforce fatigue)
- Want visibility without busywork
- Value speed and simplicity
- Team size: 5-100 sales people

**Pain Points**:
- CRM feels like a chore, not a tool
- Data entry steals selling time
- Can't find deal information quickly
- Reports take too long to build
- Legacy CRM designed for admins, not sellers

### Competitive Positioning

**Monday sales CRM is**:
- The **challenger** to Salesforce/HubSpot
- **Relatable** to salespeople (built by people who sell)
- **Honest** about what it does (no enterprise bloat)
- **Self-aware** (we know CRMs have a bad rep)

**We compete with**: Salesforce, HubSpot CRM, Pipedrive, Copper

**Positioning statements**:
- "A CRM that doesn't make you want to quit sales"
- "Built for salespeople, not CRM admins"
- "Finally, a CRM salespeople actually use"

**Self-aware**: Knows the CRM space is crowded

**Honest**: No overpromising, realistic benefits
- Specific about what we do well
- Transparent about who it's for
- Real talk, no fluff

**Relatable**: Speaks salesperson to salesperson
- Use sales language naturally
- Reference real selling scenarios
- Empathy for quota pressure

## Cross-Product Comparison

### Tone Spectrum

| Attribute | Work Mgmt | CRM | dev | service |
|-----------|-----------|-----|-----|---------|
| **Formality** | Professional | Casual+ | Technical | Professional- |
| **Boldness** | Confident | Spicy | Edgy | Energetic |
| **Playfulness** | Low | Medium | Low | Medium |
| **Technicality** | Business | Sales | High | Moderate |
| **Authority** | Expert | Challenger | Indie | Empowering |

### When to Mix Products

**Scenario**: Homepage showcasing all Monday.com products

**Approach**:
1. **Hero**: Use overarching Monday.com voice (Work Management default)
2. **Product sections**: Switch voice per product
3. **Transitions**: Use neutral Monday voice between products
4. **Closing**: Return to overarching Monday voice

**Never**: Mix product voices within a single section

### Vocabulary Differences

**Work Management vocabulary**:
- Workflows, operations, strategy, execution, alignment, scale, departments

**CRM vocabulary**:
- Pipeline, deals, quota, prospects, close, conversion, revenue

**dev vocabulary**:
- Sprints, deployments, releases, bugs, features, roadmap, ship

**service vocabulary**:
- Tickets, customers, resolution, SLA, priority, response time, agents

## Product Selection Decision Tree

Use this to determine which product voice to apply:

**1. User mentions specific product**:
- "CRM" → CRM voice
- "dev" → dev voice
- "service" → service voice
- "Work Management" → Work Management voice

**2. User mentions industry/role**:
- Sales, revenue, pipeline → CRM voice
- Developers, engineering, code → dev voice
- Support, tickets, customers → service voice
- Operations, projects, teams → Work Management voice

**3. User mentions general "Monday.com"**:
- Default to Work Management voice (flagship product)

**4. User mentions multiple products**:
- Use overarching Monday.com voice (neutral)
- Or use sequential sections with distinct voices

## Advanced: Mixing Tones Within a Product

Sometimes you need to adjust tone within a product context:

**Work Management**:
- Enterprise section: More formal, strategic
- Small team section: More accessible, friendly
- Onboarding: More helpful, patient

**CRM**:
- Competitive pages: More spicy, bold
- Feature docs: More straightforward, helpful
- Case studies: More professional, credible

**dev**:
- Landing pages: More edgy, opinionated
- Documentation: More technical, precise
- Support: More helpful, patient

**service**:
- Marketing: More energetic, empowering
- Agent training: More instructional, clear
- Reports: More analytical, professional

**Rule**: Product voice is the baseline. Adjust intensity for context, but never abandon the core character.
